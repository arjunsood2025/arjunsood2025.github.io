LLM Platform — Pretraining, Post-Training, and a From-Scratch Inference Engine

Technologies: Python, PyTorch, Triton, DDP/FSDP2, DPO, lm-eval-harness, FastAPI, Prometheus, Docker

The three systems every modern LLM lab runs — pretraining, post-training, and high-performance serving — built with no model, trainer, or serving frameworks. Every layer is in the repo: a byte-level BPE tokenizer, a LLaMA-style decoder-only transformer (RMSNorm, SwiGLU, RoPE, GQA, tied embeddings), the distributed training loop, the DPO loss, and a mini-vLLM inference engine. A 125M-parameter model was trained on 1.26B tokens at 42.7% MFU on a single RTX 5070 with bitwise-deterministic checkpoint resume.

  • Wrote two custom Triton kernels — fused RMSNorm and fused cross-entropy with online logsumexp — each with a PyTorch-reference correctness test and a microbenchmark, alongside a streaming data pipeline with MinHash deduplication and n-gram contamination filtering over memory-mapped shards.
  • Ran a 7-run scaling study across a size × token grid (10M/30M/85M), fitting the compute–loss power law L = E + A·C−α at α = 0.195 with a Chinchilla joint fit of βD = 0.323 (against the paper's 0.28), plus matched-compute architecture ablations of RoPE, SwiGLU, and GQA.
  • Post-trained with SFT (loss masking, sequence packing; 30k conversations) and a from-scratch DPO implementation (4k preference pairs, 58% preference accuracy, KL ≈ 0.10), evaluated via lm-eval-harness and a position-debiased LLM judge with bootstrap CIs — which slightly preferred the SFT model (DPO win-rate 43.8% [37.5, 48.8]), reported as measured rather than hidden.
  • Engineered a vLLM-style inference engine: paged KV cache with chained-hash prefix caching and copy-on-write (170 concurrent sequences vs. 32 for contiguous KV, 5.3×), continuous batching with recompute preemption, speculative decoding (53% draft acceptance, 3.09 output tokens per target forward at k=4), int8 weight-only quantization, and an SSE streaming server benchmarked under open-loop Poisson load.
  • Pinned correctness with 65+ CPU-only tests in CI: engine-greedy ≡ reference generate, paged attention ≡ dense SDPA on shuffled blocks, preemption cannot change output, KV-cache decode ≡ full forward, and DPO loss against hand-computed values.

Raft-Replicated Key-Value Store with a From-Scratch LSM Engine

Technologies: Rust, Tokio, Raft Consensus, LSM Trees, Deterministic Simulation Testing, serde/bincode

A linearizable, replicated key-value database built from scratch in Rust — storage engine, consensus, networking, and the test infrastructure to prove it correct. No database crates, no consensus crates, no RPC frameworks; the dependency list is tokio, serde/bincode, and crc32fast. An LSM-tree engine (CRC-framed WAL with torn-write recovery, bloom-filtered SSTables, leveled compaction, MVCC snapshots) sits beneath a sans-IO Raft core with pre-vote elections, pipelined replication, InstallSnapshot, membership changes, and ReadIndex.

  • Built a FoundationDB-style deterministic simulator that runs the entire cluster — nodes, disks, network, and clients — single-threaded on virtual time from one seeded PRNG, injecting message loss, duplication, reordering, asymmetric partitions, crash-restart with loss of volatile state, and forced snapshot transfers.
  • Executed 20,000 randomized failure schedules (≈420M simulated events, ≈17.3M client operations), asserting election safety, state-machine safety, and log matching continuously, then checking every client history with a from-scratch Wing & Gong linearizability checker; each run is a pure function of its seed and replays exactly.
  • Found and documented 8 real bugs with reproducing seeds preserved as CI regressions — the deepest a state-machine safety violation in which an InstallSnapshot covering a log prefix truncated a follower's acked entries and a later legitimate election overwrote a committed entry; five rare conditions had to align at one instant, and exactly one seed in 20,000 reaches it.
  • Shipped the production layer: a Tokio host with a single-owner event loop, group commit that batches an entire event-loop drain into one fsync, and exactly-once client sessions via replicated dedup (at-least-once delivery plus idempotent apply). Holding the workload fixed at 100% read, the price of consistency is stale 13,517 / lease 13,057 / linearizable 9,043 ops/s — linearizable reads cost ~1.5× for the ReadIndex quorum round-trip.
  • Measured failure behavior honestly: over 10 consecutive leader kills under a 32-connection load, client-observed failover ran a median of 2,141 ms — a sub-second election dominated by leader rediscovery — with 0 of 300,000 operations lost and full throughput restored ~3.5 s after the kill.

  • Wrote a C++20 limit-order-book replay engine with zero-copy pybind11/NumPy bindings and the GIL released in the hot loop, sustaining 11.5M events/sec — roughly 488× the pure-Python reference — with the standalone C++ harness reaching 15.8M events/sec at p50 100 ns / p99 200 ns per apply().
  • Enforced bit-for-bit parity between the C++ engine and a readable pure-Python reference in CI, so a bug must appear in two independent implementations to slip through; the synthetic simulator keeps its own book and tests assert the engine reconstructs that exact state from the event stream.
  • Developed an event-driven backtester modeling order latency, book-walking market impact, maker/taker fees, position limits, and L2 queue-position approximation, with the accounting identity equity = cash + position × mid and cash reconciliation against the fill log verified after every run.
  • Made leakage a failing test rather than a promise: the prefix property (features computed on truncated history must be identical to the same rows on full history) is checked mechanically, the detector is itself tested against a deliberately leaky feature, and walk-forward and purged K-fold splits are verified for overlap, ordering, and purge-gap correctness.
  • Reported every metric after costs with a stationary-bootstrap confidence interval — there is no gross-PnL code path — with the Streamlit dashboard leading on a "CI contains 0" warning when no edge is demonstrated. Time-Series ML Platform — Leakage-Safe Training, Evaluation, and Serving

Technologies: PyTorch (DDP, AMP), Transformers, TCNs, scikit-learn, OmegaConf, FastAPI, Prometheus, Docker, pytest

Leakage-safe training, evaluation, and serving for short-horizon prediction on noisy, regime-switching limit-order-book data — built as a research platform, not a trading bot. The emphasis is on measuring a signal honestly rather than flattering it, and on this data the honest answer is that the signal does not survive transaction costs, and a logistic regression matches the transformer. Both findings are reported up front rather than buried.

  • Built a config-driven training system — Transformer encoder and TCN with multi-horizon heads, focal loss with class weighting, mixed precision, gradient accumulation, cosine schedule with warmup, checkpointing, early stopping, and DDP via torchrun — predicting 3-class midprice direction at 1/5/30/60s horizons plus a forward realized-volatility bucket from 41 causal microstructure features.
  • Enforced no-lookahead by machine check: truncation invariance (features recomputed on data cut at time t are bit-identical for rows ≤ t), shuffled-label retraining that must collapse to the majority-class rate, a purge equal to the maximum 60s label horizon plus a 60s embargo, and scaler and volatility-bucket edges fit on training rows only.
  • Defended causality at the architecture level, not just in the data: the TCN normalizes per timestep rather than with GroupNorm/BatchNorm, which pool statistics across the length axis of an (N, C, L) tensor and would leak future into past through the normalization layer alone — a leak no amount of careful splitting would catch — with a receptive-field test asserting the output at step t is unchanged when every input after t is overwritten.
  • Surfaced what headline metrics hide: regime slicing showed the 0.899 volatility AUC is almost entirely regime detection, collapsing to 0.281–0.551 within regimes (below chance where 99% accuracy comes from predicting the majority class), and a transaction-cost sweep put Sharpe crossing zero at ~0.14 bps per trade — no tradable edge, stated plainly.
  • Identified that the project's own baseline comparison was invalid — 5-fold walk-forward scored against a single held-out split, which made logistic regression appear to beat the transformer (0.769 vs. 0.738) purely on easier earlier folds — and built a matched-split harness on which logistic ties the transformer at 0.738 AUC over 29,774 identical test windows. Deployed through a versioned registry with atomic promotion and a FastAPI + Prometheus service at 1.9 ms p50 single-window CPU latency and 2,774 windows/s peak throughput at batch 64; 43 pytest tests cover data, models, evaluation, and API.
